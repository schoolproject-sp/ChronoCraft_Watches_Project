Inside the Engine: Watch Calibers Explained

Every watch relies on an internal engine called a movement or caliber to regulate the passing of seconds, minutes, and hours.

Why Synthetic Rubies? Mechanical movements contain 17 to 35 tiny synthetic ruby jewels at high-friction gear pivots to prevent metal wear over decades.

Three Primary Types of Watch Movements

  • Manual-Wind Mechanical: Powered by turning the crown by hand to tighten a coiled metal mainspring, storing 40 to 80 hours of energy.
  • Automatic (Self-Winding): Uses a weighted semicircular rotor that spins naturally with the motion of the wearer's wrist to wind the mainspring automatically.
  • Quartz Electronic: Uses a small battery to send an electric current through a tiny tuning-fork-shaped quartz crystal, vibrating at exactly 32,768 times per second.
